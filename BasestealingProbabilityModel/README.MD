# **MLB Base Stealing Model - Personal Project**
**Date:** July 2026 - August 2026

## **Description**
### **Introduction**
In this project, I aim to create a machine learning model that generates the probability of base runner successfully stealing a base off a specific Pitcher and Catcher pairing. With lots of insight into the aspects that make each individual player successful in stealing or preventing stolen bases, I aim to combine those variables into a single model and identify if specific Pitcher-Catcher pairings are more vulnerable to steals. This project currently uses publicly available Statcast data from the 2023 season to train the model and create base runner profiles. However for the Shiny App, the Pitcher and Catcher Data focuses only on the 2023 Texas Rangers, a personal choice due to me being a fan and having knowledge of the players on that team. 

### **Data Collection**
The data used to train the model and create the player profiles for all three position groups came from MLB's Statcast. For the Pitcher's, I used Statcast's Search section to query for pitch by pitch data for each pitcher, specifically focusing on pitches while runners were on base. From the Pitcher data, I group the data into three pitch classifications. Fast represents Primary Pitches, Break represents Breaking Balls, and Off Represents Offspeed pitches. In each grouping, I collected the velocity of the pitch, extension of the pitcher, and calculated time the pitch took to reach the catcher as variables. For Catchers, I used the "Catcher Throwing" leaderboard and collected each individual steal attempt data from the catchers. For the catchers, I used the leaderboards normalized values for Throwing Speed and Exchange as variables. I excluded Accuracy and Teamwork due to uncertainty if these were values known before or after the play. For the runners, I used the Base Stealing Leaderboard and collected each runners individual steal data. From the runners, I use distance of their initial lead, their positioning on the first move by the pitcher, and positioning when the pitch is fully released. 

### **The Model**
The purpose of the model is to generate the probability a base stealer successfully steals their target base. With that, the model was trained on individual steal data that incorporated data from all three player groups. What makes it unique is that the model intakes data from the three pitch classifications (or less depending on the pitcher) and also displays the probability of stealing off those pitch types. The main probability is weighed depending on the specific pitcher's use of the pitch with runners on base. From the Pitchers, I use the handedness of the pitcher, pitch classification type, velocity of the pitch, extension length and time it takes the pitch to each the catcher. From the Catchers, I used the normalized values for throwing speed and exchange. From the Runners, I use length values of the initial lead, at the time of the first move, and at the time the ball is fully released. Lastly, the model also factors in the base being thrown to, labeled as 2B or 3B. 

## **Shiny App**
As a visual representation of the model's results, an interactive shiny app was created that allows you to enter the Pitcher, Catcher, Runner and Target Base. The app uses data that averages values used in the model to serve as the player's "profile". These values are then entered into the model for each pitch classification and then outputted to the application. The app also allows you to see some of the values in the profiles.
